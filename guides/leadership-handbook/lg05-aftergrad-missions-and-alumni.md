## Why This Workstream Exists

Most schools measure success at graduation.

Vision 2040 must measure success after graduation.

This workstream asks one of the most important questions in the whole programme:

What happened because this person came to Rhema?

That question turns alumni work from occasional communication into long-term stewardship.

Graduation should not be treated as the end of Rhema's responsibility. It should be treated as a handoff into ministry, calling, service, leadership and lifelong connection.

## Mission Statement

Design and validate graduate pathways, missions deployment systems, church planting support and alumni engagement structures that help Rhema graduates fulfil God's call and produce measurable Kingdom fruit.

## Strategic Context

Vision 2040 includes church planters, cross-cultural missionaries, societal leaders, supportive leaders, RMAI ministers and a strong relational alumni association.

Those outcomes cannot be assumed simply because students graduate.

They require intentional pathways, mentoring, placement support, data, relationships, care structures and feedback loops.

This workstream converts graduation into commissioning.

It also gives Rhema Nigeria a way to learn from graduates after they leave the classroom. Their experiences should inform Training, Campus Expansion, Publications, Communications, Data and future leadership development.

## Philosophy of the Workstream

Graduation is not the finish line. It is a handoff.

The goal is not to maintain a database. The goal is to help graduates keep moving towards God's purpose.

The workstream should treat every graduate as a person with a calling, not as a record in a system.

That means the work must be both relational and structured.

Relationships without systems will not scale.

Systems without relationships will not serve people well.

This workstream must hold both together.

## Core Responsibilities

The Workstream Lead is responsible for:

- mapping graduate pathways from training into ministry, work, missions and leadership;
- designing church planting support systems;
- creating missionary deployment readiness criteria;
- designing missionary care, support and re-entry protocols;
- building alumni engagement systems;
- defining graduate data requirements;
- developing graduate outcome tracking;
- creating placement, mentoring and support structures;
- coordinating with churches, mission agencies, denominations and alumni leaders;
- feeding graduate experience back into Training, Campus Expansion, Publications and Data.

## Strategic Responsibilities

Beyond task delivery, the Workstream Lead must build Rhema Nigeria's graduate success capability.

That includes:

- treating alumni as a ministry community, not a mailing list;
- helping Rhema understand where graduates are and what they are doing;
- creating segmented pathways for different callings;
- protecting missionary wellbeing through proper care systems;
- helping churches and ministry partners trust Rhema graduate referrals;
- turning graduate stories and struggles into organisational learning;
- making graduate impact visible enough to guide future decisions.

## Success Profile

The ideal Workstream Lead loves people and systems.

They remember names, build relationships and think strategically.

They understand ministry deployment, missions, alumni care and the long-term fruit of training.

They are relational without being disorganised.

They are organised without becoming cold.

They can listen to graduates, engage pastors, build trust with church networks, coordinate with internal workstreams and follow through consistently.

## Recruitment Profile

### Essential Experience

Look for someone with:

- strong relational leadership experience;
- pastoral or member-care sensitivity;
- experience supporting people after training or graduation;
- experience building communities, networks or alumni relationships;
- understanding of church planting, missions or ministry deployment;
- ability to manage records, follow-up systems and relationships together;
- practical ministry judgement;
- ability to collaborate across departments.

### Preferred Experience

Helpful experience includes:

- alumni association leadership;
- missions mobilisation or missionary care;
- church planting support;
- pastoral placement or ministry placement;
- counselling, mentoring or coaching;
- graduate data management;
- partnership development with churches, denominations or ministry networks.

### Personal Qualities

Look for someone who is:

- warm;
- faithful;
- patient;
- consistent;
- discreet;
- relationally intelligent;
- spiritually mature;
- genuinely interested in people's callings;
- able to follow up without becoming intrusive;
- able to celebrate the success of others.

### Technical Competencies

The role requires working knowledge of:

- pathway design;
- alumni engagement;
- stakeholder mapping;
- graduate outcome tracking;
- missionary care principles;
- church planting support structures;
- database discipline;
- basic reporting and segmentation.

## Who Should Not Be Appointed

Avoid appointing someone who:

- sees alumni mainly as donors;
- prefers systems over people;
- prefers people but cannot maintain systems;
- disappears into administration;
- treats all graduates the same;
- measures success only by event attendance;
- fails to follow up;
- cannot handle sensitive personal or ministry information;
- has little empathy for missionaries, church planters or young leaders in difficult seasons.

## First 90 Days

### Month One

- Understand Vision 2040 graduate-related goals.
- Review existing graduate records.
- Meet key alumni, pastors, ministry partners and internal workstream leads.
- Map existing alumni relationships and deployment stories.
- Identify gaps in graduate data quality.

### Month Two

- Segment graduate pathways.
- Define early graduate outcome measures.
- Draft alumni engagement principles.
- Begin mapping potential church planting, missions and placement partners.
- Define information that must be captured before graduation.

### Month Three

- Draft church planting, missions and alumni engagement frameworks.
- Propose graduate pathway categories.
- Develop an initial missionary care model.
- Present graduate data requirements to Data & Reporting.
- Present first recommendations to the PMO and Executive Leadership.

The Workstream Lead may design pathway frameworks, engagement checklists, alumni segmentation methods, graduate data requirements and draft deployment tools.

## Success Indicators

The workstream is succeeding when:

- graduates feel remembered and supported;
- churches request Rhema graduates;
- mission placements become healthier;
- alumni mentor younger graduates;
- graduate stories inform organisational learning;
- Rhema can locate and understand its graduate impact;
- church planters know where to get support;
- missionaries receive care before crisis;
- Training receives useful feedback from graduates;
- alumni engagement becomes more than events.

## Common Mistakes

- Losing contact after graduation.
- Treating all graduates alike.
- Becoming event-driven.
- Measuring newsletters instead of fruit.
- Underestimating missionary care.
- Treating alumni as a funding pool before serving them as people.
- Failing to capture graduate learning for the rest of the organisation.
- Building a database that nobody keeps current.

## Advice from the PMO

People are not projects.

Your work is to keep graduates connected to purpose, support and opportunity.

Do not let the database replace relationship.

Do not let relationship become so informal that Rhema loses institutional memory.

Serve alumni first.

Support missionaries before they burn out.

Help church planters before their struggles become emergencies.

Listen to graduates. They are telling Rhema what its training produced.

## Annual Leadership Rhythm

### Weekly

- Follow up on active graduate support needs.
- Review pending placement, missions or church planting matters.
- Maintain contact with key alumni and partner relationships.

### Monthly

- Review graduate data quality.
- Track alumni engagement.
- Review missionary and church planter support needs.
- Meet with Training and Data representatives.
- Capture graduate stories and lessons.

### Quarterly

- Review pathway outcomes.
- Review KPI progress.
- Update risk register for graduate deployment and missionary care.
- Review alumni engagement by segment.
- Report graduate insights to the PMO.

### Annually

- Review graduate outcomes by cohort.
- Review missionary retention and health.
- Review church planting outcomes.
- Update graduate pathway frameworks.
- Produce an annual graduate impact learning report.

## Assumptions and Open Questions

### Assumptions

- Rhema Nigeria intends to maintain structured contact with graduates beyond graduation.
- Graduate pathways will include ministry, missions, church planting, supportive leadership and societal leadership.
- Alumni engagement will be designed to serve graduates before asking graduates to support Rhema.
- Data & Reporting will support graduate outcome tracking.

### Open Questions

- What definition should Rhema Nigeria use for an actively engaged alumnus?
- What qualifies as graduate placement within six months?
- How should missionary retention be measured across different mission contexts?
- What data can be realistically collected from graduates without overwhelming them?
- How will this workstream interface with RMAI?
- Should church planting and missions support include financial support, training support, mentoring support or all three?

### Decisions Required

- Approve graduate pathway categories.
- Approve the minimum graduate data set.
- Define alumni engagement levels.
- Define missionary care responsibilities.
- Define how church planting support will be approved and tracked.
- Decide whether Graduate Success Framework should cover the first three years, five years or longer.

## Letter to My Successor

If you are reading this, never let the database replace the relationship.

The records matter because the people matter.

Serve alumni first.

Listen to their stories.

Learn from their struggles.

Help each graduate keep taking the next faithful step.

Some will plant churches.

Some will go to difficult places.

Some will serve quietly behind others.

Some will lead in society.

Your task is to help Rhema Nigeria remember them, support them, learn from them and celebrate the fruit God brings through them.

Keep the work personal.

Keep the system disciplined.

Both are necessary.
