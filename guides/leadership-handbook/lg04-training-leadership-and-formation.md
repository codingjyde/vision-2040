# Leadership Guide 04: Training, Leadership & Formation

## Purpose of this Guide

This guide defines the role of the leader responsible for designing the training ecosystem for Vision 2040.

The richer working name is Training, Leadership & Formation because Rhema is not only transferring information. It is forming people.

## Why This Workstream Exists

If Campus Expansion determines where Rhema grows, this workstream determines what kind of leaders Rhema produces.

Vision 2040 depends on the quality of graduates.

## Mission Statement

Design and validate a training and formation system that produces faithful, skilled, Spirit-led leaders able to serve churches, communities and nations.

## Strategic Context

Vision 2040 includes 100,000 leaders trained, Kainos, Kids & Youth Academies, campus expansion, missions and church planting.

These goals require consistent curriculum, prepared faculty, clear assessment and strong formation pathways.

## Philosophy of the Workstream

Start with graduate outcomes.

Do not ask only what courses should be taught. Ask what kind of graduate Rhema Nigeria must produce.

Training should preserve Rhema DNA while preparing leaders for practical ministry and life.

## Core Responsibilities

- Design curriculum architecture across programme levels.
- Define delivery formats and quality standards.
- Establish instructor requirements.
- Create faculty readiness criteria.
- Design teaching session and instructor readiness checklists.
- Create student assessment frameworks.
- Define graduation readiness and certification standards.
- Build feedback loops from graduates and ministry partners.

## Key Deliverables

### 2026

- Curriculum Architecture
- Delivery Format Standards
- Instructor Requirements
- Draft Teaching Checklist
- Graduate Competency Framework

### 2027

- Validated Teaching Checklist
- Assessment Checklist
- Faculty Readiness Confirmation
- Certification Standards
- Training Quality Assurance Package

## Success Profile

The ideal lead understands both doctrine and learning design.

They are not merely a gifted teacher. They can design learning journeys, develop faculty, evaluate outcomes and preserve standards at scale.

## Recruitment Profile

Look for someone who has:

- academic or training leadership experience;
- deep respect for Rhema doctrine;
- ability to design curriculum;
- faculty development experience;
- understanding of adult learning;
- practical ministry experience;
- comfort with digital and blended learning;
- ability to listen to graduate feedback.

## Who Should Not Be Appointed

Avoid someone who:

- thinks information equals transformation;
- measures quality only by exams;
- resists improving existing courses;
- ignores faculty development;
- treats curriculum as static;
- adds content without asking whether it forms leaders.

## First 90 Days

Month One: review current curriculum, faculty and programme levels.

Month Two: define graduate outcomes and competencies.

Month Three: draft curriculum architecture, faculty criteria and validation approach.

## Working Relationships

Reports to: Vision 2040 Executive Leadership.

Collaborates with: Campus Expansion, AfterGrad, Publications, Data, Communications and Organisation Design.

Depends on: Publications for learning resources and Data for outcome measurement.

## Decision Authority

May design curriculum frameworks, checklists and faculty readiness criteria.

Recommends certification standards, assessment standards and faculty development priorities.

Requires approval for major curriculum changes and certification policy.

## Team Structure

Recommended team:

- Training Workstream Lead
- Academic Leader
- Curriculum Designer
- Faculty Representative
- Assessment Specialist
- Digital Learning Specialist
- AfterGrad Representative
- Publications Representative

## Success Indicators

The workstream is succeeding when:

- faculty teach with consistency;
- graduates are useful in ministry;
- churches trust Rhema graduates;
- students are shaped, not merely examined;
- curriculum reflects both doctrine and practical ministry;
- feedback from graduates improves the training system.

## Common Mistakes

- Adding more content instead of improving formation.
- Teaching for exams rather than ministry.
- Underinvesting in faculty.
- Ignoring the hidden curriculum of leadership behaviour.
- Assuming past methods will automatically serve future growth.

## Advice from the PMO

Every lesson eventually becomes someone's ministry.

Design training that still bears fruit twenty years after graduation.

## Vision 2040 Goals Supported

This workstream directly supports the training of leaders, campus expansion, Kainos, Kids & Youth Academies, church planting, missions, societal leadership and supportive leadership.

## Legacy

The Workstream Lead should leave behind a graduate competency framework, curriculum architecture, faculty readiness system, assessment standards, training checklists and quality assurance process.

## Letter to My Successor
