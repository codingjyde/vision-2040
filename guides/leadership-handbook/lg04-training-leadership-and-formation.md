## Purpose of this Guide

This guide defines the leadership expectations, responsibilities, operating philosophy and success profile for the person appointed to lead Workstream 04, Training & Leadership Development.

The working name used in this guide is Training, Leadership & Formation. That name is not a formal workstream rename. It clarifies the deeper burden of the role: Rhema Nigeria is not merely transferring information. It is forming leaders.

This guide supports recruitment, appointment, onboarding, coaching, performance review, succession planning and knowledge transfer.

## Source References

This guide should be read alongside the source documents listed in Document Control.

The Workstream KPI Framework Summary is the baseline for formal KPIs. The wider success indicators in this guide are leadership observations. They should not be treated as approved KPIs unless later adopted by leadership.

## Why This Workstream Exists

If Campus Expansion determines where Rhema grows, this workstream determines what kind of leaders Rhema produces.

Vision 2040 depends on the quality of graduates. Campuses, publications, missions, church planting and alumni engagement all become stronger when training is sound, consistent and deeply formative.

This workstream exists to design and validate the training ecosystem required for future expansion. It must protect Rhema DNA while preparing students for faithful, skilled, Spirit-led ministry in Nigeria, Africa and beyond.

## Mission Statement

Design and validate a training and formation system that produces faithful, skilled, Spirit-led leaders who can serve churches, communities and nations while preserving Rhema Nigeria's doctrine, values and ministry standards.

## Strategic Context

The 2026-2027 restructuring phase is the time to build training quality before expansion increases complexity.

Vision 2040 includes training 100,000 leaders, establishing campuses across Nigeria, expanding through Kainos, developing Kids and Youth Academies, supporting church planting, sending missionaries and raising societal leaders.

Those goals require more than a list of courses. They require curriculum architecture, faculty development, delivery standards, assessment frameworks, certification standards and feedback loops from graduates and ministry partners.

This workstream depends heavily on Campus Expansion, AfterGrad, Publications, Data and Organisation Design. It also serves them. A strong training system feeds every other Vision 2040 outcome.

## Philosophy of the Workstream

Start with graduate outcomes.

Do not ask only, "What courses should we teach?" Ask, "What kind of graduate must Rhema Nigeria produce if Vision 2040 is to become reality?"

Training is not the same as information transfer. Leadership is formed through teaching, modelling, practice, correction, responsibility and feedback.

The message must remain faithful. The methods can improve.

The workstream should therefore hold two commitments together:

- preserve Rhema doctrine, DNA and standards;
- improve delivery, faculty development, assessment and formation pathways.

This is not a contradiction. It is stewardship.

## Core Responsibilities

The Workstream Lead is responsible for:

- designing curriculum architecture across programme levels;
- defining delivery formats and quality standards;
- establishing instructor requirements and faculty readiness criteria;
- developing teaching session and instructor readiness checklists;
- designing student assessment frameworks;
- defining graduation readiness and certification standards;
- building feedback loops from graduates, faculty, churches and ministry partners;
- aligning training outputs with Vision 2040 pathways;
- coordinating with Publications on learning resources;
- coordinating with Data & Reporting on graduate outcomes and training quality.

## Strategic Responsibilities

Beyond completing training documents, the Workstream Lead must build a system that can protect quality as the organisation grows.

This includes:

- defining the graduate competency framework;
- identifying what must remain non-negotiable across every campus;
- distinguishing curriculum content from delivery method;
- designing a faculty development pathway;
- creating assessment that measures readiness for ministry, not only memory;
- ensuring digital, modular, residential and extension models carry the same standards;
- ensuring alumni feedback improves future training;
- preparing the training system for multi-campus execution from 2028.

The deeper work is formation. The lead must help Rhema Nigeria shape people, not merely certify them.

## Success Profile

The ideal Workstream Lead understands both doctrine and learning design.

They are not merely a gifted teacher. They can design learning journeys, develop faculty, evaluate outcomes and preserve standards at scale.

They value the classroom, but they do not limit formation to the classroom. They understand that students are also shaped by examples, culture, assignments, feedback, ministry opportunities, leadership behaviour and relationships.

They can earn faculty trust while still raising standards.

They know the difference between protecting the message and protecting old methods.

## Recruitment Profile

### Essential Experience

Look for someone with:

- academic or training leadership experience;
- deep respect for Rhema doctrine and ministry heritage;
- practical ministry experience;
- experience developing or reviewing curriculum;
- ability to work with faculty and instructors;
- understanding of assessment and certification;
- ability to design systems for more than one campus;
- clear writing and documentation ability.

### Preferred Experience

Helpful but not essential experience includes:

- adult learning or instructional design;
- digital or blended learning;
- faculty development;
- quality assurance in education or ministry training;
- programme accreditation or certification work;
- alumni or graduate outcome tracking;
- multi-campus teaching operations.

### Personal Qualities

The person should demonstrate:

- spiritual maturity;
- doctrinal steadiness;
- teachability;
- patience;
- humility with faculty;
- courage to improve weak systems;
- ability to listen to graduates;
- love for students;
- commitment to excellence without harshness.

### Technical Competencies

The role requires competence in:

- curriculum mapping;
- competency frameworks;
- assessment design;
- faculty readiness criteria;
- learning outcomes;
- teaching quality review;
- training documentation;
- quality assurance.

## Who Should Not Be Appointed

Avoid appointing someone who:

- thinks information equals transformation;
- measures quality only by examinations;
- resists improving existing courses;
- ignores faculty development;
- treats curriculum as static;
- adds content without asking whether it forms leaders;
- values academic polish more than ministry fruit;
- cannot work respectfully with senior faculty;
- treats digital learning as either a threat or a magic answer.

## First 90 Days

### Month One

- Review current curriculum across programme levels.
- Meet academic leaders, faculty representatives and programme leaders.
- Review existing teaching, assessment and graduation standards.
- Study graduate feedback where available.
- Identify obvious training quality risks.

### Month Two

- Define the graduate outcomes and draft the graduate competency framework.
- Map existing courses against desired competencies.
- Identify curriculum gaps, overlaps and overload.
- Draft instructor readiness and teaching quality criteria.
- Begin discussing delivery format standards.

### Month Three

- Draft curriculum architecture.
- Draft the teaching checklist and validation plan.
- Draft the assessment framework outline.
- Present key decisions required to leadership.
- Agree the 2026-2027 workplan with the PMO.

## Success Indicators

The workstream is succeeding when:

- faculty teach with greater consistency;
- graduates are useful in ministry;
- churches trust Rhema graduates;
- students are shaped, not merely examined;
- curriculum reflects both doctrine and practical ministry;
- feedback from graduates improves the training system;
- programme leaders can explain the desired graduate outcomes clearly;
- faculty development becomes normal, not corrective;
- training quality can be maintained across future campuses.

## Common Mistakes

- Adding more content instead of improving formation.
- Teaching for exams rather than ministry.
- Underinvesting in faculty.
- Ignoring the hidden curriculum of leadership behaviour.
- Assuming past methods will automatically serve future growth.
- Treating digital learning as a shortcut around faculty quality.
- Allowing every campus or instructor to drift into private standards.
- Confusing student satisfaction with training effectiveness.

## Advice from the PMO

Every lesson eventually becomes someone's ministry.

Every faculty standard eventually shapes a classroom.

Every assessment eventually tells a student what Rhema values.

Design training that still bears fruit twenty years after graduation.

Protect the message. Develop the teachers. Listen to graduates. Keep improving the system without losing Rhema DNA.

## Assumptions and Open Questions

### Assumptions

- Rhema Nigeria will retain core doctrinal and training standards while improving delivery and validation.
- Faculty development will be treated as a normal part of quality assurance.
- Graduate outcomes will inform future curriculum review.
- Digital and blended learning may support Vision 2040, but will not replace the need for qualified instructors.

### Open Questions

- What are the official minimum faculty requirements for each programme level?
- Which curriculum elements are non-negotiable across all campuses and formats?
- What delivery formats will be approved for Kainos and Kids & Youth Academies?
- How will alumni and graduate feedback be collected and reviewed?
- What level of digital learning is acceptable within Rhema Nigeria standards?

### Decisions Required

- Approve the graduate competency framework.
- Approve faculty readiness criteria.
- Approve assessment and certification standards.
- Approve delivery format standards for residential, modular, online and extension training.
- Decide how often curriculum and faculty standards should be reviewed.

## Letter to My Successor

If you are reading this, remember that training quality determines ministry fruit.

Do not treat curriculum as a file to protect. Treat it as seed to steward.

Guard the message. Honour the Word. Develop the teachers. Listen to the graduates. Watch the fruit.

Some people will ask for more content. Others will ask for easier standards. Your task is not to please either group. Your task is to help Rhema Nigeria produce leaders who are faithful, skilled, Spirit-led and useful in the harvest.

Improve the system, but do not lose the DNA.
