## Purpose of this Guide

This guide defines the leadership expectations, responsibilities, operating philosophy and success profile for the person appointed to lead Workstream 03, Campus Expansion Model.

It supports recruitment, appointment, onboarding, coaching, performance review, succession planning and knowledge transfer.

This is not a conventional job description. It is a leadership guide for the person entrusted with designing the model that will make future campus expansion safe, repeatable and faithful.

## Why This Workstream Exists

Vision 2040 includes establishing 40 campuses across Nigeria, including four campuses in Lagos and at least one Rhema Nigeria campus in every state and the FCT.

That cannot be done safely by improvising each launch.

This workstream exists to make campus expansion repeatable, measurable and faithful to Rhema DNA.

Its assignment is not to open campuses during the 2026-2027 restructuring phase. Its assignment is to design, document and validate the model that makes future campus launches possible from 2028 onwards.

## Mission Statement

Design, document and validate a campus multiplication system that enables Rhema Nigeria to launch and sustain healthy campuses from 2028 onwards.

## Strategic Context

The 2026-2027 restructuring phase is for model design and validation. No campus should be launched merely because there is enthusiasm, opportunity or pressure.

Expansion should begin only after the operating model, staffing assumptions, financial assumptions, checklists and launch gates have been tested.

This workstream prepares the practical engine for future expansion. It converts the Vision 2040 campus goal into an operating model that can be understood, taught, repeated and improved.

The workstream depends strongly on:

- Governance & Leadership, for launch gates and approval protocols.
- Organisation Design & Capability, for campus roles and reporting lines.
- Training, Leadership & Formation, for curriculum and faculty readiness.
- Finance, Stewardship & Sustainability, for launch budgets and sustainability assumptions.
- Data, Insights & Reporting, for campus health metrics and dashboard reporting.
- Communications, Brand & Engagement, for stakeholder engagement and campus mobilisation.

## Philosophy of the Workstream

Do not design a hero model.

Design a system ordinary faithful leaders can operate well.

A campus model that depends on unusually gifted individuals will not scale. A healthy model should help capable, submitted and well-trained leaders succeed without forcing them to invent everything from scratch.

The goal is not one impressive launch. The goal is a campus multiplication system that improves with each use.

This workstream should think in stages:

1. Exploration.
2. Readiness.
3. Launch.
4. Stabilisation.
5. Growth.
6. Multiplication.

Launching is not the finish line. A campus that opens well but struggles in years two and three has not yet proved the model.

## Core Responsibilities

The Workstream Lead is responsible for:

- designing the campus operating model;
- defining location selection criteria and scoring methodology;
- developing campus staffing models, role definitions and competency requirements;
- creating the Campus Launch Checklist with the required launch elements;
- creating weekly operations and monthly performance checklists;
- modelling campus financial assumptions and sustainability requirements;
- designing the pilot or validation methodology;
- documenting evidence-based findings from the pilot;
- capturing lessons learned for future campus leaders;
- preparing the 2028 launch readiness package.

## Strategic Responsibilities

Beyond producing documents, the Workstream Lead must:

- resist premature expansion pressure;
- protect Rhema DNA while allowing responsible local adaptation;
- design for campus health, not merely campus openings;
- ensure financial assumptions are tested before launch;
- identify what must be centralised and what can be localised;
- create a model that can eventually serve campuses beyond the first wave of expansion;
- help leadership understand the true cost, risk and readiness requirements of campus multiplication.

## Success Profile

The ideal Workstream Lead builds systems, not spectacles.

They understand operations, finance, ministry standards and people. They can resist premature expansion pressure. They are patient enough to validate before scaling.

They are not satisfied with a plan that looks good on paper. They want evidence that the model can work in real Nigerian contexts, with real staff, real budgets, real students and real constraints.

They can honour the excitement of expansion without being ruled by it.

## Who Should Not Be Appointed

Avoid appointing someone who:

- loves launching but dislikes maintenance;
- improvises everything;
- thinks every campus must be unique;
- rushes ahead of readiness gates;
- measures success only by number of campuses opened;
- treats financial sustainability as an afterthought;
- resists documentation;
- dismisses the need for pilot testing;
- believes enthusiasm is a substitute for readiness.

## First 90 Days

### Month One

- Study the Vision 2040 campus goal.
- Review existing Rhema Nigeria campus experience.
- Study international Rhema launch patterns where available.
- Meet current campus leaders and administrators.
- Identify recurring campus strengths, weaknesses and risks.

### Month Two

- Map the campus lifecycle from exploration to multiplication.
- Draft the first version of the campus operating model.
- Draft location selection criteria.
- Begin defining staffing, finance and operational assumptions.
- Identify the minimum viable launch checklist.

### Month Three

- Validate the draft model with Governance, Finance, Training and Data workstreams.
- Draft the pilot or simulation plan.
- Identify evidence required before 2028 launch approval.
- Present the first model review to the PMO and executive leadership.

## Success Indicators

The workstream is succeeding when:

- a prospective Campus Director knows exactly what is required;
- leadership can explain the campus launch pathway clearly;
- common launch risks are identified early;
- every new campus can follow the same clear pathway;

- launch decisions are based on evidence rather than pressure;
- the model improves through pilot feedback;
- campus quality standards remain clear across locations.

## Common Mistakes

- Copying the first successful campus rather than extracting principles.
- Measuring launches instead of healthy campuses.
- Over-engineering documents until ordinary leaders cannot use them.
- Ignoring what fails in years two and three.
- Treating local adaptation as compromise.
- Treating financial sustainability as a finance-only issue.
- Ignoring campus closure, pause or merger criteria.
- Allowing enthusiasm to bypass evidence.

## Advice from the PMO

Your job is not to open campuses during the restructuring phase.

Your job is to make campus expansion safe, repeatable and faithful.

Every campus should teach the next campus how to do better.

Do not confuse confidence with evidence. A launch model is not validated because leaders like it. It is validated when the checklists, assumptions and operating rhythms have been tested.

Protect the gates. Protect the standards. Protect the future Campus Directors who will inherit the model you design.

## Assumptions and Open Questions

### Assumptions

- No new campus launches are expected during the 2026-2027 restructuring phase unless leadership explicitly changes the phase boundary.
- The campus model will be validated before the 2028 implementation phase.
- Existing campus experience and international Rhema launch patterns can inform the model, but should not be copied without contextual adaptation.
- The financial model must be validated before launch approval.

### Open Questions

- What are the ten required elements of the Campus Launch Checklist?
- What is the minimum campus size for financial and academic viability?
- Which standards are non-negotiable across all campuses, and which can be locally adapted?
- What are the criteria for pausing, merging or closing a campus?
- Should campuses be classified by maturity level?
- What facility standards are required for launch, and what can improve after launch?

### Decisions Required

- Approve the location selection criteria.
- Approve the launch gate process.
- Approve the pilot or simulation methodology.
- Approve the campus maturity model, if adopted.
- Approve the minimum financial sustainability threshold for launch.

## Letter to My Successor

If you are reading this, protect the model from impatience.

Expansion is exciting. Premature expansion is expensive.

Do not allow pressure, sentiment or opportunity to bypass readiness. A campus that opens too early may damage the very vision it was meant to serve.

Keep the gates clear. Keep the standards faithful. Keep learning from each launch.

Remember that a campus is more than a venue, timetable and signboard. It is a training environment. It carries Rhema's message, culture and trust.

Build the kind of model that future leaders can use with confidence. Then keep improving it as evidence grows.
