## Purpose of this Guide

This guide defines the expectations for the leader appointed to Workstream 06, Finance & Funding.

The working name, Finance, Stewardship & Sustainability, clarifies the deeper purpose of the assignment. The role is not merely to track money. It is to help Rhema Nigeria build financial credibility, funding capacity and long-term sustainability for Vision 2040.

This guide supports recruitment, appointment, onboarding, coaching, performance review, succession planning and knowledge transfer.

## Why This Workstream Exists

Vision 2040 requires more than accounting.

It requires stewardship, financial planning, funding credibility, reporting discipline and sustainability.

The central question is:

How do we fund a movement for the next fifteen years?

A good finance system does not simply pay bills. It helps leadership make wise decisions, protects trust, shows the true cost of the vision and ensures that growth does not outrun stewardship.

## Mission Statement

Design the financial architecture, funding model, reporting system and stewardship practices required to support Vision 2040 responsibly, transparently and sustainably.

## Strategic Context

The 2026-2027 restructuring phase should establish the financial foundations needed for the 2028 implementation phase.

This workstream must prepare:

- budget architecture for restructuring and implementation;
- financial tracking systems;
- diversified funding models;
- monthly reporting protocols;
- campus funding checklists;
- financial assumptions for future expansion;
- clear evidence that the 2028 rollout can be funded responsibly.

Finance must support the vision without becoming reckless or fearful.

If finance is weak, expansion becomes unstable. If finance becomes controlling, ministry slows down. The right balance is disciplined faithfulness.

## Philosophy of the Workstream

Good stewardship is not merely spending less.

It is achieving the right outcomes with what God has entrusted.

Money follows trust. Trust grows through clarity, accountability, transparency and measurable results.

This workstream should therefore help Rhema Nigeria become financially credible. That means leaders, partners, donors and future funders should be able to see:

- what the vision requires;
- what each initiative costs;
- what has already been committed;
- what gaps remain;
- what risks exist;
- what outcomes the funding will produce.

The role is not to say no by default.

The better question is:

How can this be done responsibly?

## Core Responsibilities

The Workstream Lead is responsible for:

- designing budget frameworks for the restructuring phase and 2028 implementation reserves;
- developing a diversified funding model;
- creating financial tracking systems;
- designing financial reporting templates and monthly reporting protocols;
- coordinating budget requirements across all workstreams;
- creating Campus Funding Checklist and Monthly Reporting Checklist tools;
- supporting donor, partner and leadership reporting;
- identifying major cost assumptions across the Vision 2040 goals;
- presenting financial status to leadership in a clear, timely and useful way;
- developing sustainability assumptions for 2028 and beyond.

## Strategic Responsibilities

Beyond the technical finance work, this leader should:

- make the vision financially understandable;
- help leadership compare options with evidence;
- distinguish capital costs from recurring operating costs;
- design early-warning signals for budget pressure;
- build financial confidence among partners and donors;
- help workstreams think in total cost, not only launch cost;
- support scenario planning for conservative, expected and accelerated growth;
- ensure finance supports mission without losing accountability.

This role should help Rhema Nigeria understand the economics of Vision 2040.

## Success Profile

The ideal Workstream Lead is a financial steward, not merely a bookkeeper.

They combine precision with vision.

They can explain numbers clearly to non-financial leaders. They understand that every Naira represents sacrifice, trust and responsibility.

They are not afraid of ambition, but they do not confuse ambition with affordability. They help leaders see what can be done now, what must be phased and what requires fresh funding.

They see finance as a ministry enabler.

## Recruitment Profile

### Essential Experience

Look for someone with:

- strong financial management experience;
- budget design and monitoring experience;
- financial reporting competence;
- ability to build simple financial models;
- experience explaining financial information to non-financial leaders;
- proven integrity with money;
- comfort working with executive leadership;
- ability to meet reporting deadlines consistently.

### Preferred Experience

- wisdom;

## Who Should Not Be Appointed

Avoid appointing someone who:

- sees finance only as bookkeeping;
- cannot explain numbers simply;
- uses finance to block ministry unnecessarily;
- avoids innovation;
- hides bad news;
- confuses cost-cutting with stewardship;
- delays reports until they are no longer useful;
- treats every expense the same;
- has weak integrity or unclear handling of money;
- prefers pleasing people to reporting accurately.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating funding as fundraising only.
- Ignoring operating costs after launch.
- Reporting too late for decisions.
- Failing to separate capital and recurring costs.
- Avoiding scenario planning.
- Treating every workstream request as equally urgent.
- Measuring spending without measuring outcomes.
- Allowing budget conversations to become disconnected from the Vision 2040 goals.

## Advice from the PMO

You are not protecting money.

You are protecting ministry.

Every Naira represents someone's sacrifice. Spend carefully, report truthfully and invest with faith and wisdom.

Do not let fear make you small.

Do not let excitement make you careless.

Help leaders see clearly. Good financial clarity gives courage to act wisely.

## Annual Leadership Rhythm

### Weekly

- Review urgent financial issues.
- Track high-risk budget items.
- Support workstreams with active financial questions.
- Update critical funding assumptions where needed.

### Monthly

- Produce financial status report.
- Review budget variance.
- Update funding pipeline.
- Meet with PMO and Data & Reporting.
- Escalate material risks.

### Quarterly

- Review financial readiness against workstream milestones.
- Update forecast assumptions.
- Review donor and partner reporting needs.
- Reassess funding coverage.
- Present strategic finance summary to leadership.

### Annually

- Review the full budget framework.
- Capture financial lessons learned.
- Update the Vision Economics Model.
- Prepare the next implementation budget package.
- Review sustainability assumptions for 2028 and beyond.

## Assumptions and Open Questions

### Assumptions

- Finance & Funding remains a temporary restructuring workstream, not a permanent department name.
- The internal finance function will continue to exist alongside this workstream.
- Final budget approval remains with executive leadership.
- Data & Reporting will support dashboard and reporting requirements.
- Some funding streams may require donor, partner or grant reporting beyond ordinary internal reports.

### Open Questions

- What accounting or financial tracking system will be used during the restructuring phase?
- Who owns donor relationship management versus financial reporting to donors?
- What reserve policy should guide the 2028 implementation phase?
- Which Vision 2040 goals require separate business cases?
- What level of budget variance triggers escalation?
- How will restricted funds be tracked and reported?
- What non-donor revenue streams should be developed first?

### Decisions Required

- Approve the budget framework format.
- Approve the monthly reporting format.
- Decide the financial tracking system for 2026-2027.
- Define approval thresholds for expenditure commitments.
- Decide whether each Vision 2040 goal should have a separate financial model.
- Define the minimum funding readiness threshold for 2028 implementation.
