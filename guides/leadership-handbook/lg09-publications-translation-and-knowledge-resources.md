## Purpose of this Guide

This guide defines the leadership expectations, responsibilities, operating philosophy and success profile for the person appointed to lead Workstream 09, Publications & Translation.

The working name, Publications, Translation & Knowledge Resources, is broader than the official workstream name. It reflects the strategic role of this workstream in preserving, organising and multiplying Rhema Nigeria's teaching, publications, translations and reusable organisational knowledge.

This is not a conventional job description. It is a leadership guide.

## Why This Workstream Exists

Publications are a multiplication strategy.

A teacher can reach a class. A book, translation, study guide, audio resource or digital resource can reach people the teacher may never meet.

Vision 2040 includes the distribution of Faith Library Publications, translation into Hausa and later other Nigerian languages, training resources, campus resources and long-term knowledge preservation.

This workstream exists to preserve and multiply Rhema's voice.

## Mission Statement

Design the publication, translation, distribution and knowledge resource systems that preserve Rhema doctrine, support training and extend ministry impact across languages, regions and generations.

## Strategic Context

The 2026-2027 restructuring phase is a preparation phase.

This workstream should not merely produce more material. It should design the systems that help Rhema Nigeria decide what to publish, what to translate, how to review it, how to distribute it and how to preserve it for future use.

The workstream supports Training, Campus Expansion, AfterGrad, Missions, Media, Communications and Governance by turning knowledge into trusted, reusable resources.

If every workstream creates its own documents without coordination, Rhema Nigeria will accumulate scattered content. This workstream should help prevent that.

## Philosophy of the Workstream

Publishing is stewardship.

The goal is not simply to print books. The goal is to preserve, organise, translate and distribute truth faithfully.

Translation is ministry, not merely linguistics.

A faithful translation must be accurate, readable, culturally intelligible and doctrinally sound.

This workstream should ask:

- What ministry need does this resource solve?
- Who is the intended reader or learner?
- Has the content been reviewed for doctrinal accuracy?
- Can this resource be reused across campuses and programmes?
- How will the source files, rights, terminology and revision history be preserved?
- How will distribution reach both urban centres and remote locations?

## Core Responsibilities

The Workstream Lead is responsible for:

- developing publication priorities;
- designing translation workflows;
- creating translation quality gates;
- defining distribution models;
- creating Translation Checklist and Distribution Checklist tools;
- managing author, translator, publisher and distribution relationships;
- supporting workstreams with manuals, guides and learning resources;
- preserving institutional knowledge in reusable form;
- maintaining quality standards for multilingual resources;
- coordinating with Training, AfterGrad and Media so resources match programme needs.

## Strategic Responsibilities

Beyond producing publications, this leader must build a publishing and knowledge-resource capability.

That includes:

- preserving Rhema doctrine through careful review;
- ensuring local language work is accurate and culturally clear;
- protecting intellectual property and rights records;
- creating terminology standards for translation;
- preventing duplicated or disconnected resource creation;
- supporting future digital publishing and resource access;
- helping the organisation treat knowledge as a strategic asset.

## Success Profile

The ideal Workstream Lead sees content strategically.

They value accuracy, doctrine, readability, translation quality and long-term preservation.

They understand that today's resource may shape lives decades from now.

They are neither careless with speed nor paralysed by perfection. They know how to move important work forward while preserving quality.

They can work with authors, translators, reviewers, designers, publishers, distributors and ministry leaders without losing the purpose of the work.

## Recruitment Profile

Look for someone with:

- publishing or editorial experience;
- strong writing or editing judgement;
- theological sensitivity;
- project coordination ability;
- quality-control discipline;

- respect for intellectual property and source materials;

The person should be:

- careful;
- patient;

- organised;
- teachable;
- faithful with details;
- comfortable receiving review;
- willing to protect quality under pressure.

### Technical Competencies

The role benefits from competence in:

- editorial workflows;

## Who Should Not Be Appointed

Avoid appointing someone who:

- values speed over accuracy;
- treats translation as a final afterthought;
- resists digital formats;
- dislikes collaboration;
- publishes because material exists rather than because it solves a ministry need;
- ignores version control, rights and source files;
- cannot receive correction from theological reviewers;
- treats distribution as less important than production.

## First 90 Days

### Month One

- Review existing publications, rights, translations and source files.
- Identify current resource gaps for Training, Campus Expansion, AfterGrad and Media.
- Meet key stakeholders, including faculty, publications staff, translators, distributors and Communications.
- Review current publication and translation standards.

### Month Two

- Define publication priorities.
- Draft translation workflow.
- Identify language and distribution priorities.
- Begin a rights, terminology and source-file inventory.
- Define review gates for doctrine, language, readability and cultural clarity.

### Month Three

- Draft quality standards.
- Draft distribution model.
- Draft Knowledge Resource Map.
- Prepare initial Translation Checklist and Distribution Checklist.
- Present early recommendations to the PMO and executive leadership.

The workstream is succeeding when:

- students use quality Rhema resources;

- translations are trusted by native speakers and ministry leaders;
- publications support missions, church planting and campus expansion;

## Common Mistakes

- Measuring books printed instead of books used.
- Treating translation as a technical task only.
- Neglecting revision and updates.
- Losing rights, source files or translation memory.
- Allowing each workstream to create disconnected resources.
- Treating distribution as an afterthought.
- Failing to involve native speakers and doctrinal reviewers early enough.

## Advice from the PMO

Do not think of yourself merely as a publisher.

Think of yourself as a steward of truth and knowledge.

Publish carefully. Translate faithfully. Preserve wisely.

A resource can outlive its production team. Treat every approved resource as something future leaders may depend on.

## Assumptions and Open Questions

### Assumptions

- Rhema Nigeria will need both print and digital resource strategies.
- Hausa translation remains the first major translation priority under Vision 2040.
- Publications and translation require formal theological review before broad release.
- Distribution strategy must cover both urban centres and remote regions.

### Open Questions

- Which publications have current rights cleared for local printing, translation or digital distribution?
- Who gives final doctrinal approval for translated materials?
- Which Hausa dialect, orthography and terminology standards should be used?
- Which other Nigerian language groups should follow Hausa, and by what criteria?
- Should the Rhema Knowledge Centre sit under this workstream, Data & Reporting, or the PMO after 2027?

### Decisions Required

- Approve publication priority criteria.
- Approve translation review process and final approval authority.
- Approve source-file and version-control standards.
- Decide whether to build a formal Rhema Knowledge Centre during or after the restructuring phase.
- Decide the distribution model for Northern Nigeria and remote ministry contexts.

## Letter to My Successor

If you are reading this, remember that the resources you steward may outlive all of us.

Protect the message.

Honour the authors.

Serve the readers.

Respect translators.

Keep the knowledge organised so future leaders do not have to start again.

Translate with care.
