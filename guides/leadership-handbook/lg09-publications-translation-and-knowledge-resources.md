# Leadership Guide 09: Publications, Translation & Knowledge Resources

## Purpose of this Guide

This guide defines the role of the leader responsible for publications, translation and long-term knowledge resources within Vision 2040.

## Why This Workstream Exists

Publications are a multiplication strategy.

A teacher can reach a class. A book, translation or digital resource can reach people the teacher may never meet.

This workstream preserves and multiplies Rhema's voice.

## Mission Statement

Design the publication, translation, distribution and knowledge resource systems that preserve Rhema doctrine, support training and extend ministry impact across languages and generations.

## Strategic Context

Vision 2040 includes the distribution of Faith Library Publications, translation into Hausa and other languages, training resources, campus resources and long-term knowledge preservation.

This workstream supports every other workstream by turning knowledge into usable resources.

## Philosophy of the Workstream

Publishing is stewardship.

The goal is not simply to print books. The goal is to preserve, organise, translate and distribute truth faithfully.

Translation is ministry, not merely linguistics.

## Core Responsibilities

- Develop publication priorities.
- Design translation workflows.
- Create translation quality gates.
- Define distribution models.
- Create translation and distribution checklists.
- Manage author, translator, publisher and distribution relationships.
- Support workstreams with manuals, guides and resources.
- Preserve institutional knowledge in reusable form.

## Key Deliverables

### 2026

- Publication Priority Matrix
- Translation Workflow
- Quality Review Process
- Knowledge Resource Map
- Draft Translation Checklist

### 2027

- Validated Translation Checklist
- Distribution Model
- Distribution Checklist
- Publications Calendar
- Knowledge Centre Proposal
- Translation and Style Standards

## Success Profile

The ideal lead sees content strategically.

They value accuracy, doctrine, readability, translation quality and long-term preservation. They understand that today's resource may shape lives decades from now.

## Recruitment Profile

Look for someone who has:

- publishing or editorial experience;
- theological sensitivity;
- translation management awareness;
- project coordination ability;
- respect for intellectual property;
- interest in digital publishing;
- ability to develop authors and reviewers;
- commitment to quality.

## Who Should Not Be Appointed

Avoid someone who:

- values speed over accuracy;
- treats translation as a final afterthought;
- resists digital formats;
- dislikes collaboration;
- publishes because material exists rather than because it solves a ministry need;
- ignores version control and rights.

## First 90 Days

Month One: review existing publications, rights, translations and resource gaps.

Month Two: define publication priorities and translation workflow.

Month Three: draft quality standards, distribution model and knowledge resource plan.

## Working Relationships

Reports to: Vision 2040 Executive Leadership.

Collaborates with: Training, AfterGrad, Campus Expansion, Communications, Data and Finance.

This workstream should serve as the publishing partner for all workstreams.

## Decision Authority

May design publication priorities, translation workflows, checklists and quality standards.

Recommends major translation investments, publication sequencing and distribution partnerships.

Requires approval for major publication investments, doctrinal review standards and rights decisions.

## Team Structure

Recommended team:

- Publications Lead
- Editor
- Theological Reviewer
- Translation Coordinator
- Linguist or Native Speaker Reviewers
- Graphic Designer
- Digital Publishing Specialist
- Rights or Copyright Adviser
- Distribution Specialist

## Success Indicators

The workstream is succeeding when:

- students use quality Rhema resources;
- faculty can find approved materials easily;
- translations are trusted;
- publications support missions and church planting;
- resources are version-controlled;
- institutional knowledge is preserved rather than scattered.

## Common Mistakes

- Measuring books printed instead of books used.
- Treating translation as a technical task only.
- Neglecting revision and updates.
- Losing rights, source files or translation memory.
- Allowing each workstream to create disconnected resources.

## Advice from the PMO

Do not think of yourself merely as a publisher. Think of yourself as a steward of truth and knowledge.

Publish carefully. Translate faithfully. Preserve wisely.

## Vision 2040 Goals Supported

This workstream directly supports Faith Library distribution, Hausa translation, training, Kainos, Kids & Youth Academies, campus expansion, missions, media and long-term leadership development.

## Legacy

The Workstream Lead should leave behind publication priorities, translation workflows, quality standards, distribution models, terminology guidance, rights records, resource archives and the foundation for a Rhema Knowledge Centre.

## Letter to My Successor

If you are reading this, remember that the resources you steward may outlive all of us.

Protect the message. Honour the authors. Serve the readers. Keep the knowledge organised so future leaders do not have to start again.
