## Purpose of this Guide

This guide defines the expectations for the leader appointed to Workstream 08, Media & Communications.

The working name, Communications, Brand & Engagement, is used because the role is broader than media production. It must build understanding, trust, alignment and participation around Vision 2040.

This guide supports recruitment, appointment, onboarding, coaching, performance review, succession planning and knowledge transfer.

## Why This Workstream Exists

Vision 2040 will not succeed merely because it has been documented.

People must understand it.

They must believe it.

They must know where they fit.

This workstream exists to design the communication architecture that helps Rhema Nigeria's internal and external stakeholders understand the vision, trust the process and participate with confidence.

Its work is not limited to social media, graphics or event publicity.

It exists to build shared understanding.

## Mission Statement

Design the messaging, brand, storytelling, internal communication and engagement systems that help stakeholders understand Vision 2040 and participate with confidence.

## Strategic Context

The 2026-2027 restructuring phase will create questions.

People will ask:

- What is changing?
- Why are we restructuring?
- What does this mean for campuses, staff, students, alumni and partners?
- How can I participate?
- Is the organisation making progress?

If these questions are not answered clearly, people will create their own answers.

This workstream therefore supports alignment, confidence and change readiness.

It must serve every other workstream by turning progress, decisions, lessons and proof points into clear communication.

It must also help leaders communicate the same vision in their own voices without fragmenting the message.

## Philosophy of the Workstream

Communication is leadership.

The goal is not to publish more content. The goal is to shape understanding, confidence and participation.

Media production matters, but media production is not the same as communication strategy.

Every leader communicates.

Every meeting communicates.

Every delay communicates.

Every report communicates.

This workstream designs the system that helps the organisation communicate with clarity, honesty and consistency.

It should keep asking:

- What do stakeholders need to understand now?
- What question are we answering?
- What trust are we building?
- What action should this communication encourage?
- What story makes the vision tangible?
- What must be communicated internally before it is communicated externally?

## Core Responsibilities

The Workstream Lead is responsible for:

- developing the Vision 2040 messaging framework;
- defining audience segments and stakeholder communication needs;
- designing internal communication rhythms;
- designing content planning, approval and publishing workflows;
- creating content, campaign and weekly publishing checklists;
- establishing a communication calendar;
- developing a Vision 2040 narrative framework;
- building story pipelines across all workstreams;
- monitoring communication effectiveness using engagement metrics;
- preparing crisis communication protocols;
- supporting leaders with speeches, updates, articles, presentations and stakeholder briefings;
- preserving communication assets for future use.

## Strategic Responsibilities

Beyond producing content, this leader must build the communication capability required for long-term expansion.

This includes:

- making Vision 2040 easy to explain;
- strengthening internal alignment during restructuring;
- equipping leaders to communicate consistently;
- helping partners see progress and impact;
- ensuring communication supports donor confidence;
- ensuring the Rhema Nigeria voice remains recognisable across channels;
- preventing message drift as more campuses and programmes emerge;
- capturing stories that show what God is doing through the ministry.

The workstream should become the architect of the Vision 2040 community, not merely the publisher of Vision 2040 updates.

## Success Profile

The ideal Workstream Lead is a strategic storyteller.

They write clearly.

They understand people.

They protect brand consistency without making every communication sound stiff.

They know how to help leaders communicate in a way that feels authentic, clear and aligned.

They are not merely creative. They are disciplined.

They are not merely visible. They are trusted.

They understand that communication can reduce resistance, strengthen confidence and help people carry the vision together.

## Recruitment Profile

### Essential Experience

Look for someone with:

- communications strategy experience;
- strong writing ability;
- content planning experience;
- audience segmentation experience;
- stakeholder communication experience;
- experience managing approval workflows;
- experience with internal and external communication;
- ministry sensitivity.

### Preferred Experience

Helpful experience includes:

- brand management;
- campaign planning;
- speechwriting or executive communication;
- crisis communication;
- media relations;
- digital publishing;
- storytelling through video, photography or long-form writing;
- communication analytics.

### Personal Qualities

Look for:

- clarity;
- discernment;
- calm judgement;
- patience;
- emotional intelligence;
- attention to detail;
- respect for leadership voice;
- ability to listen before writing;
- ability to simplify complexity;
- sensitivity to timing.

### Technical Competencies

The lead should understand:

- messaging architecture;
- editorial calendars;
- content workflows;
- brand standards;
- channel strategy;
- audience journeys;
- engagement metrics;
- content quality review;
- basic digital publishing systems.

## Who Should Not Be Appointed

Avoid appointing someone who:

- confuses communication with graphics;
- chases trends constantly;
- measures success only by likes and followers;
- communicates activities without outcomes;
- ignores internal communication;
- cannot write clearly;
- publishes before listening;
- enjoys visibility more than clarity;
- handles pressure poorly;
- makes every issue sound like promotion;
- avoids difficult but necessary messages.

## First 90 Days

### Month One

- Review existing channels, audiences and messaging.
- Meet all Workstream Leads.
- Identify internal communication gaps.
- Map key stakeholder groups.
- Review current brand, media and publication standards.
- Identify immediate communication risks in the restructuring phase.

### Month Two

- Draft the Vision 2040 messaging framework.
- Draft audience journeys for priority stakeholder groups.
- Design the internal communication rhythm.
- Define content categories and story types.
- Draft the content approval workflow.

### Month Three

- Draft the communication calendar.
- Draft the content, campaign and weekly publishing checklists.
- Create the first story pipeline across workstreams.
- Present the communication framework for PMO and leadership review.
- Identify the first 90-day communication priorities.

- audience segmentation model;

- major campaign strategies;
- crisis communication protocol;
- public communication priorities;
- leadership communication support needs.

### Requires Approval For

Approval is required for:

- major public campaigns;
- crisis statements;

## Common Mistakes

- Communicating activities instead of outcomes.
- Only communicating success.
- Becoming reactive.
- Ignoring internal audiences.
- Allowing inconsistent voice across channels.
- Treating communication as decoration after decisions are made.
- Overproducing content without building understanding.
- Confusing trend-following with relevance.
- Waiting for rumours before communicating clearly.

## Advice from the PMO

Your work is not to fill social media.

Your work is to help people understand and carry the vision.

Communication builds trust before trust is needed.

Do not exaggerate.

Do not hide difficulty.

Tell the truth with wisdom.

Make the vision plain enough for people to repeat and practical enough for them to join.

## Annual Leadership Rhythm

### Weekly

- Review content pipeline.
- Publish according to the approved rhythm.
- Capture updates and story leads from workstreams.
- Support urgent communication needs.

### Monthly

- Review communication calendar.
- Review engagement metrics.
- Gather proof points from Data & Reporting.
- Review internal communication needs.
- Update leadership on message risks and audience feedback.

### Quarterly

- Conduct brand consistency review.
- Review audience engagement trends.
- Refresh narrative priorities.
- Review communication effectiveness with PMO.
- Update story archive and campaign lessons.

### Annually

- Review the messaging framework.
- Refresh audience segmentation.
- Review crisis communication readiness.
- Produce an annual communication learning report.
- Update communication templates and checklists.

## Assumptions and Open Questions

### Assumptions

- The workstream will support both internal and external communication.
- The workstream will coordinate with existing media, branding and marketing structures rather than replace them without approval.
- Leadership will provide timely decisions and approvals for major messages.
- Data & Reporting will provide evidence for progress updates.

### Open Questions

- Which communication channels are official for Vision 2040?
- What is the approval process for public communications?
- Who owns the Rhema Nigeria brand standards during restructuring?
- How will crisis communication authority be defined?
- How will internal communication reach campuses, staff, faculty, students, alumni and partners consistently?
- What baseline will be used for engagement improvement?

### Decisions Required

- Approve the Vision 2040 messaging framework.
- Approve audience segmentation priorities.
- Approve the publishing rhythm.
- Approve crisis communication protocol.
- Decide whether this workstream owns brand standards or only applies them for Vision 2040.
